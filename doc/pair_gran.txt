pair_style gran command :h3
pair_style bubble command :h3
pair_style gran_bubble bubble command :h3

[Syntax:]

pair_style gran model_type model_name model_keyword model_value :pre
pair_style bubble model_type model_name model_keyword model_value :pre
pair_style gran_bubble model_type model_name model_keyword model_value :pre

zero or more model_type/model_name pairs may be appended. They must be appended in the following order (!) :l
  {model} values = described "here"_Section_gran_models.html
  {tangential} values = described "here"_Section_gran_models.html
  {cohesion} values = described "here"_Section_gran_models.html
  {rolling_friction} values = described "here"_Section_gran_models.html
  {surface} values = described "here"_Section_gran_models.html :pre
following the model_type/model_name pairs, zero or more model_keyword/model_valuezero pairs may be appended in arbitrary order :l
  model_type/model_name pairs = described for each model separately "here"_Section_gran_models.html  :pre
in addition, the following keywords of the pair style itself may be appended :l
  {history_clear_legacy} values = {on} or {off}
    {off} (default) = the contact history of a pair is reset as soon as the pair is beyond the contact distance
    {on} = keep it until the next neighbor list build (behaviour of earlier versions, see below)
  {multicontact_radius_legacy} values = {on} or {off}
    {off} (default) = with surface {multicontact}, both particles of a contact use their per-contact expanded radius
    {on} = particle i uses its unexpanded radius (behaviour of earlier versions, see below)
  {synchronized_verlet} values = {on} or {off}
    {off} (default) = pair contacts use the normal at the current (full-step) positions
    {on} = pair contacts use the normal at the half-step positions, consistent with the half-step velocities (see below) :pre
:ule

[Examples:]

pair_style gran model hooke tangential history
pair_style gran model hertz tangential history rolling_friction cdt
pair_style gran model hertz tangential no_history cohesion sjkr
pair_style gran model hertz tangential history history_clear_legacy on
pair_style gran model hertz tangential history synchronized_verlet on  :pre

[General description:]

The {gran} styles imposes a force between two neighboring particles. Typically, there is
a force when the distance r between two particles of radii Ri and Rj is less than their
contact distance dist = Ri + Rj, and no force otherwise. Some models, such as cohesion
models, may impose a force also when the particle surfaces do not touch. This is documented
for those models specifically.


The general form of a granular interaction can be written as:

:c,image(Eqs/pair_gran_html_60b8ced2.png)

The quantities in the equations are as follows:

delta_n = d - r = overlap distance of 2 particles  :ulb,l
k_n = elastic constant for normal contact :l
k_t = elastic constant for tangential contact :l
gamma_n = viscoelastic damping constant for normal contact :l
gamma_t = viscoelastic damping constant for tangential contact :l
delta_t = tangential displacement vector between 2 spherical particles :l
:ule

In the first term is the normal force between the two particles and the second term is the
tangential force. The normal force has 2 terms, a contact force and a damping force. The
tangential force also has 2 terms: a shear force and a damping force. The shear force is
a "history" effect that accounts for the tangential displacement ("tangential overlap")
between the particles for the duration of the time they are in contact.

The concrete implementation for k_n, k_t, gamma_n, gamma_t and the shear history
depend on the concrete models as chosen by the user. They are described on separate
doc pages "here"_Section_gran_models.html

Also, other models may add additional forces or torques on the particles, such as
cohesive or rolling friction forces. These are also described on separate
doc pages "here"_Section_gran_models.html

The styles {bubble} and {gran_bubble} are aliases for {gran}, which can e.g. be used
for the modelling of systems with different phases using "pair hybrid"_pair_hybrid.html, where a
different set of interaction laws is used for each phase. An example would be 

pair_style hybrid gran        model hertz     tangential history &
                  bubble      model hertz     tangential off &
                  gran_bubble model hertz     tangential off :pre

IMPORTANT NOTE: The order of model keywords is important, you have to stick
to the order as outlined in the "Syntax" section of this doc page.

[General comments:]

For granular styles there are no additional coefficients to set for each pair of atom types
via the "pair_coeff"_pair_coeff.html command. All settings are global and are made via the
pair_style command. However you must still use the "pair_coeff"_pair_coeff.html for all pairs
of granular atom types. For example the command

pair_coeff * * :pre

should be used if all atoms in the simulation interact via a granular potential
(i.e. one of the pair styles above is used). If a granular potential is used as a sub-style
of "pair_style hybrid"_pair_hybrid.html, then specific atom types can be used in the pair_coeff
command to determine which atoms interact via a granular potential.


[Contact history of separating pairs:]

The contact history of a pair (tangential spring, rolling spring, maximum overlap,
...) is reset to zero as soon as the pair is evaluated beyond its contact distance,
i.e. when neither the particle surfaces touch nor the pair is within the distance at
which models act without contact ("neigh_modify contact_distance_factor"_neigh_modify.html,
set automatically by e.g. cohesion {easo/capillary/viscous}, {washino/capillary/viscous}
and {jkr}). Inside that distance each model keeps or resets its own history as
documented for it (a liquid bridge keeps its history until it ruptures; tangential
{history} resets its spring when the surfaces separate). The same reset is done for
particle-wall contacts by "fix wall/gran"_fix_wall_gran.html, and by the neighbor list
build for pairs beyond the contact distance. A pair that touches again therefore starts
from zero history, and the result does not depend on when the neighbor list is
rebuilt: it equals a run with "neigh_modify every 1 delay 0 check no"_neigh_modify.html
(tests/x05).

IMPORTANT NOTE: Up to the phase-D version of the LIGGGHTS modernization branch, the
history of a separated pair was kept until the next neighbor list build (finding X-05).
A pair that touched again within one neighbor interval (rebounds, vibrated beds,
rattlers) re-used the old tangential and rolling springs, and the results depended on
the neighbor list settings and on run boundaries. Results of such simulations change.
{history_clear_legacy on} restores the old behaviour. A warning is printed once
(rank 0, after the run in question: at the setup of the next run or at the end of
the input, so that it does not interrupt the thermo output) the first time the
corrected default can change a result, i.e. when a pair
whose non-zero history was reset touches again before the next neighbor list build
(in rare cases the models overwrite the reset values before using them, and the
result is unchanged although the warning is printed).

IMPORTANT NOTE: With surface {multicontact}, the per-contact expanded radius
(fix multicontact/halfspace) was applied to particle j of a pair only, while
particle i used its unexpanded radius, so the contact depended on which particle
of the pair was "i" (this changes with atom sorting, re-neighboring and the number
of processors). Both particles now use their expanded radius.
{multicontact_radius_legacy on} restores the old behaviour. A warning is printed
once when the corrected default is in use.

{synchronized_verlet on} follows Vyas et al. ("(Vyas)"_#Vyas) and LAMMPS
pair granular: velocity-Verlet evaluates the forces of a step with the
positions x(n+1) but the half-step velocities v(n+1/2). For pair contacts
the normal is then taken at the half-step positions,
n = (dx - dt/2 vr) / |dx - dt/2 vr| with dx = xi - xj and vr = vi - vj,
and used for the normal and tangential relative velocities, the normal
force and the torques. The tangential history is incremented with this
relative velocity and then projected onto the plane of the full-step
normal. Wall contacts are unchanged. The tangential dashpot uses the
relative velocity in the half-step plane (LAMMPS rotates it into the
full-step plane; the difference is of the order of the rotation of the
normal within one step).

Without it, a fine particle rolling over coarse ones does not leave
them where it should at size ratios above about 3. Test: a fine sphere
rolls off two fixed spheres 7 times larger (friction 10, rolling
without slip, released 20 degrees from the top). The rigid analytic
separation angle is 66.5 degrees. The default gives 74.1 degrees, and
69.7 with a 4 times smaller time step (first order in dt); with
{synchronized_verlet on} it gives 68.5 degrees at both time steps (the
remaining difference is the elastic contact, not the integration).

{synchronized_verlet on} requires spherical particles, surface
{default}, tangential {history} (without {tangential_rotate},
{tangential_rescale} or {tangential_incremental}) or {no_history},
cohesion {off}, {sjkr} or {sjkr2}, and rolling_friction {off}, {cdt} or
{epsd2}. It cannot be combined with the {velocity_predictor} keyword of
"fix nve/sphere"_fix_nve_sphere.html. It is not applied in the setup
of a run or for "compute pair/gran/local"_compute_pair_gran_local.html,
where positions and velocities are both at the full step. With
"package omp"_package.html the serial pair kernel is used.

The keywords {history_clear_legacy}, {multicontact_radius_legacy} and
{synchronized_verlet} are not stored
in restart files; re-issue the pair_style command after "read_restart"_read_restart.html
to use them.

[Mixing, shift, table, tail correction, restart, rRESPA info:]

The "pair_modify"_pair_modify.html mix, shift, table, and tail options are not
relevant for granular pair styles.

These pair styles write their information to "binary restart files"_restart.html,
so a pair_style command does not need to be specified in an input script that reads a restart file.

IMPORTANT NOTE: The material properties are not written to restart files! Thus, if you
restart a simulation, you have to re-define them (by using the fixes mentioned above).

These pair styles can only be used via the pair keyword of the "run_style respa"_run_style.html
command. They do not support the inner, middle, outer keywords.

[Newton's 3rd law ("newton"_newton.html):]

Both settings of the pairwise newton flag are supported, also for models with contact
history (tangential {history}, rolling friction {epsd}, {epsd2}, {epsd3}, {luding},
cohesion {jkr}, normal {hooke/hysteresis}, {luding}, {edinburgh},
{edinburgh/stiffness}, {thornton_ning}, surface {superquadric}):

{newton off} (the setting of all example inputs): a pair whose two particles are owned
by different processors is computed on both processors, and each keeps its own copy of
the contact history. :ulb,l
{newton on}: every pair is computed once; the force and torque on the partner are
added to its ghost copy and summed on the owner by reverse communication. The contact
history is stored once per pair. When atoms are exchanged, the history records of pairs
whose partner was a ghost atom are sent back to the owner of that atom, so the history
survives re-neighboring, migration to another processor, a change of the processor that
computes the pair, and restart files (which can be read with either newton setting).
This saves the duplicate computation of pairs across processor boundaries. :l
:ule

Results with {newton on} and {newton off} agree to round-off, not bitwise, because the
sums over contacts are done in a different order (tests/newton).

With {newton on}, the following options stop with an error, because they write per-contact
or per-particle data of ghost atoms that is not reverse-communicated, or because their
history values do not transform to the partner's view by a sign change; use {newton off}
for them:

cohesion {easo/capillary/viscous} and {washino/capillary/viscous} (liquid transfer), :ulb,l
{computeDissipatedEnergy} and {computeElasticPotential}, :l
surface {multicontact}, and any other history value that has not
been verified for {newton on} (the error message names it), :l
storage of per-contact forces (fix multisphere, fix continuum/weighted, multicontact),
the per-particle sum of normal forces and energy tracking, :l
a granular style with contact history used as a sub-style of "pair hybrid"_pair_hybrid.html. :l
:ule

[Restrictions:]

These pair styles require that atoms store torque and angular velocity (omega) as
defined by the "atom_style"_atom_style.html. They also require a per-particle radius is
stored. The {sphere} or {granular} atom style does all of this.

This pair style requires you to use the "communicate vel yes"_communicate.html option so that
velocites are stored by ghost atoms.

Only unit system that are self-consistent (si, cgs, lj) can be used with this pair style.

Velocity-dependent contact forces (dashpots) use the half-step velocity of the
velocity-Verlet integrator by default, which makes damped contacts converge at
first order in the timestep. The {velocity_predictor} keyword of
"fix nve/sphere"_fix_nve_sphere.html restores second-order convergence.
