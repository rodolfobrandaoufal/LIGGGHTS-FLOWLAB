:line

fix nve/sphere command :h3

[Syntax:]

fix ID group-ID nve/sphere :pre

ID, group-ID are documented in "fix"_fix.html command :ulb,l
nve/sphere = style name of this fix command :l
zero or more keyword/value pairs may be appended :l
keyword = {update} or {velocity_predictor} :l
  {update} value = {dipole}
    dipole = update orientation of dipole moment during integration
  {velocity_predictor} value = {no} or {normal} or {full} or {yes}
    no = contact forces use the half-step velocity (default)
    normal = predict the normal relative velocity of contacts
    full = predict the translational and angular velocities (= yes) :pre
:ule

[Examples:]

fix 1 all nve/sphere
fix 1 all nve/sphere update dipole
fix 1 all nve/sphere velocity_predictor full :pre

[Description:]

Perform constant NVE integration to update position, velocity, and
angular velocity for finite-size spherical particles in the group each
timestep.  V is volume; E is energy.  This creates a system trajectory
consistent with the microcanonical ensemble.

This fix differs from the "fix nve"_fix_nve.html command, which
assumes point particles and only updates their position and velocity.

If the {update} keyword is used with the {dipole} value, then the
orientation of the dipole moment of each particle is also updated
during the time integration.  This option should be used for models
where a dipole moment is assigned to particles via use of the
"atom_style dipole"_atom_style.html command.

The {velocity_predictor} keyword (opt-in, default {no}) improves the
time accuracy of velocity-dependent contact forces of "pair_style
gran"_pair_gran.html and "fix wall/gran"_fix_wall_gran.html: normal
and tangential dashpots and the viscous terms of cohesion models.
Velocity-Verlet evaluates the forces of step n+1 with the half-step
velocity v(n+1/2) = v(n) + dt/2 a(n), so a dashpot c*v is evaluated
half a step too early. Damped contacts then converge only first order
in the time step (V-04): the error of the contact time and of the
coefficient of restitution halves when dt halves. With the keyword the
fix stores dt/2 a(n) (and dt/2 alpha(n) for {full}) per particle,
communicates it to ghost particles, and the contact kernels pass the
predicted velocity v(n+1/2) + dt/2 a(n) = v(n+1) + O(dt^2) to the
velocity-dependent terms. Positions, the integration itself and all
forces that do not depend on velocity are unchanged.

{normal}: only the normal relative velocity of a contact is predicted
(normal dashpot, viscous normal cohesion). Works with all contact
models of spherical particles.

{full} (or {yes}): the relative translational and rotational
velocities are predicted, so the tangential dashpot also uses them.
The tangential spring of "tangential history"_gran_tangential_history.html
keeps the displacement-consistent half-step increment vtr(n+1/2)*dt;
predicting it would add a first-order bias. Requires surface model
{default} and tangential model {history} or {no_history}; use {normal}
otherwise.

Measured with head-on collisions (hooke, hertz, luding; e = 0.1, 0.5,
0.9; dt = tH/25 to tH/400; averaged over the contact-onset phase):
contact time error order 1.0 without and 1.9 to 2.0 with the
predictor; restitution error order 0.4 to 1.0 without and 1.3 to 2.1 with it
(the errors at tH/400 are 17 to 60 times smaller for e <= 0.5). The
linear dashpot of hooke and luding jumps at contact onset and at
separation, which leaves a first-order error whose sign depends on
where the contact starts within a time step; it averages out over many
contacts but can dominate single collisions. In a sticking oblique
impact the tangential and spin errors at tH/400 are halved with {full}
and are 1.2 to 1.4 times larger with {normal}. Tangential results
remain first order because the shear spring starts with a full
increment vtr*dt in the step where the contact begins.

Notes: the predictor is inactive in the setup of a run and for
"compute pair/gran/local"_compute_pair_gran_local.html (velocities are
synchronized there). Particles not integrated by this fix (other
integrators, frozen particles) contribute no prediction. For strongly
damped contacts the stability limit of the time step is smaller: for
a linear contact with damping ratio zeta = 0.5 / 1 / 2 the largest
stable dt*omega0 is 0.83 / 0.48 / 0.25 with the predictor against
1.24 / 0.83 / 0.48 without (omega0 = sqrt(k/m)), i.e. about half for
over-damped contacts such as viscous liquid bridges. The cost is one
forward communication of 3 ({normal}) or 6 ({full}) values per
particle and step and a few operations per contact. Only one
integrator fix may use the keyword; run_style respa and non-spherical
particles are not supported.

:line

[Restart, fix_modify, output, run start/stop, minimize info:]

No information about this fix is written to "binary restart
files"_restart.html.  None of the "fix_modify"_fix_modify.html options
are relevant to this fix.  No global or per-atom quantities are stored
by this fix for access by various "output
commands"_Section_howto.html#howto_8.  No parameter of this fix can
be used with the {start/stop} keywords of the "run"_run.html command.
This fix is not invoked during "energy minimization"_minimize.html.

[Restrictions:]

With {velocity_predictor}, the fix creates an internal "fix
property/atom"_fix_property.html named velocityPredictorDv ({normal})
or velocityPredictorDvDw ({full}); it is not written to restart files
and is re-initialized in the first step of a run.

This fix requires that atoms store torque and angular velocity (omega)
and a radius as defined by the "atom_style sphere"_atom_style.html
command.  If the {dipole} keyword is used, then they must also store a
dipole moment as defined by the "atom_style dipole"_atom_style.html
command.

All particles in the group must be finite-size spheres.  They cannot
be point particles.

[Related commands:]

"fix nve"_fix_nve.html, "fix nve/asphere"_fix_nve_asphere.html

[Default:] velocity_predictor = no
