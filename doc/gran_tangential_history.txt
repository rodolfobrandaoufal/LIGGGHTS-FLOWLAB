:line

gran tangential history model :h3

[Syntax:]

tangential history \[other model_type/model_name pairs as described "here"_pair_gran.html \] keyword values :pre
zero or more keyword/value pairs may be appended to the end (after all models are specified) :l
  {heating_tangential_history} values = 'on' or 'off'
    on = model contributes to surface heating in the frame of "surface sphere/heatable"_gran_surface_sphere_heatable.html
    off = model does not contributes to surface heating
  {tangential_rescale} values = 'on' or 'off'
    on = project the stored spring onto the current tangent plane and restore its length
    off = project only (legacy; the spring shrinks when the normal turns)
  {tangential_rotate} values = 'on' or 'off'
    on = rotate the stored spring with the mean spin of the pair (particle-particle contacts)
    off = no rotation (legacy)
  {tangential_incremental} values = 'on' or 'off'
    on = incremental Mindlin force with rescaling on unloading (Thornton et al. 2013)
    off = total-form spring F_t = -k_t(delta) * u_t (legacy)
  {coulomb_total} values = 'on' or 'off'
    on = Coulomb limit applied to the total tangential force (spring + damping)
    off = Coulomb limit applied to the spring force only (legacy)
  {twisting_marshall} values = 'on' or 'off'
    on = twisting resistance about the contact normal (Marshall model, as LAMMPS pair granular)
    off = no twisting resistance (legacy) :pre

[Description:]

This granular model is based on the general description of granular
force interaction as described in "pair gran"_pair_gran.html.

The spring part of the tangential force (F_t = k_t * u_t) is a "history" effect
that accounts for the tangential displacement ("tangential overlap") between the
particles for the duration of the time they are in contact.  The tangential
overlap (u_t) is calculated by integrating the releative tangential velocity at
the contact point over time.

If this model is chosen, then this "tangential overlap" spring force is
actually calculated / taken into account.

The coefficient of friction cof is the upper limit of the tangential force through
the Coulomb criterion Ft_spring = cof*Fn, where Ft_spring and Fn are the tangential 
spring and total normal force components. Thus in the Hookean case, the tangential force
between 2 particles grows according to a tangential spring and dash-pot model until
Ft_spring/Fn = cof and is then held at Ft_spring = Fn*cof until the particles lose contact.
In the Hertzian case and other non-linear cases, a similar analogy holds, though the 
spring is no longer linear.

The damping contribution is only added in time-steps where there is no slip,
i.e. the Coulomb criterion is not met.

[Frame-indifferent and incremental options (opt-in):]

All four options below default to 'off'. With the defaults, results are
bitwise identical to earlier versions. They can be given to both
"pair gran"_pair_gran.html and "fix wall/gran"_fix_wall_gran.html. When one is
on, a one-line summary is printed on rank 0.

{tangential_rescale on}: in each step, the stored tangential displacement u_t is
first projected onto the current tangent plane. It is then rescaled to the
length it had before the projection, and only then is the increment
v_t,rel*dt added. The legacy update projects without rescaling, so the spring
loses a fraction of about (1-cos(phi)) of its length every time the contact
normal turns by phi. This is the approach of Luding (2008, eq. 17), Thornton et
al. (2013, eq. 18), and LAMMPS GRANULAR (rotate_rescale_vec).

{tangential_rotate on}: before the projection, u_t is rotated rigidly with the
mean spin of the pair, w = (omega_i + omega_j)/2, by the angle |w|*dt about the
axis w/|w| (Rodrigues formula). The component of w along the normal is the
twist of the contact about its normal. For two particles spinning together
about the normal, the legacy spring stays fixed in the lab frame, so its
direction in the particles' frame changes with time. The tangential part of w
carries the spring along when the normal itself turns. With this option, the
update is exact for a pair moving as a rigid body: the contact force stays
constant in the body frame to round-off. The rotation applies to
particle-particle contacts only. Wall contacts keep the wall (lab) frame. A
rotating mesh (fix move/mesh rotate) is therefore not frame-indifferent.
Frame indifference needs both options: {tangential_rescale on} {tangential_rotate on}.

{tangential_incremental on}: the tangential force is updated incrementally,
dF_t = -k_t(delta) du_t, instead of F_t = -k_t(delta) u_t. When k_t
decreases (unloading), the force is scaled by k_t/k_t,old (Thornton et al.
2013; LAMMPS mindlin_rescale/force). Internally the spring is still stored as a
displacement; it is multiplied by k_t,old/k_t while k_t increases. The option
adds one history value ("kt_old") per contact. It has an effect only when k_t
depends on the overlap (hertz). With hooke, k_t is constant and the option
changes nothing.

Energy note: for any tangential law in which F_t depends on delta while F_n does
not depend on u_t, a closed elastic contact cycle is not conservative. The
reason is that dW = F_n d(delta) + F_t du_t is not an exact differential unless
k_t is constant. The legacy total form can therefore *create* energy: up to
+0.75 % in an elastic (e=1), sticking, oblique sphere-plane impact. The
incremental form with the unloading rule never creates energy. Instead it
dissipates energy, like Mindlin-Deresiewicz micro-slip: -1.4 % at 5 degrees
and -18 % at 80 degrees for the same impact with mu=10. Neither form conserves
energy to 0.1 %.

{coulomb_total on}: the Coulomb limit mu*|F_n| is applied to the total
tangential force (spring + dashpot). The spring is then back-computed as
u_t = -(F_t + gamma_t v_t,rel)/k_t, as in LAMMPS GRANULAR. In the legacy
update, the damping is added after the spring has been capped. The total
|F_t| can therefore exceed mu*|F_n| for strongly damped contacts with a small
normal force. With {heating_tangential_history on}, the heat source is the
damping power plus the elastic energy released by slip.

The four options cannot be combined with {computeElasticPotential} or
{computeDissipatedEnergy}; such an input stops with an error. Restart files
store the contact history. A run that uses {tangential_incremental on} has one
extra history value per contact, so a restart must keep the same setting for
{tangential_incremental}.

{twisting_marshall on}: adds a torque about the contact normal that
resists the relative spin w_n = (omega_i - omega_j).n (Marshall 2009, as
"twisting marshall" in LAMMPS pair granular). The twist angle
phi += w_n dt is stored per contact, and the torque on particle i is
M n with M = -k_tw phi - gamma_tw w_n, limited to |M| <= M_crit, where

k_tw = k_t a^2/2, gamma_tw = gamma_t a^2/2, M_crit = 2/3 a mu |F_n|, :pre

k_t, gamma_t and mu are the tangential stiffness, damping and friction
coefficient of the contact, and a = sqrt(delta_n R*) is the contact radius.
When the limit is reached the stored angle is reset so that the torque
equals the limit (sliding). Particle j gets -M n. For wall contacts the
spin of the wall is taken as zero. Without this option a spinning
sphere on a plane keeps spinning forever, since no other force law in
LIGGGHTS-PUBLIC acts about the contact normal (the {luding} rolling
model has a {torsion} option of its own).

Test (tests/twist): a sphere at rest on a plane or on a fixed sphere,
given a spin of 20 rad/s about the vertical, decelerates at
M_crit/I to within 1e-4 (sphere-plane 91.5 rad/s^2, sphere-sphere
72.6 rad/s^2), then sticks and rings around zero spin on the twist
spring. One extra history value per contact is used, so a restart must
keep the same setting.

This model contributes to surface heating in the frame of 
"surface sphere/heatable"_gran_surface_sphere_heatable.html if the 
appropriate flag is activated (only available in the PREMIUM version).

[Coarse-graining information:]

Using "coarsegraining"_coarsegraining.html in
combination with this command might lead to
different dynamics or system state and thus to 
inconsistancies. However, the influence of this
model on the global dynamics or system state might 
be small so in some cases the results may be valid. 
This has to be reviewed by a specialist on a 
case-by-case basis.

NOTE: "Coarsegraining"_coarsegraining.html may or 
may not be available in LIGGGHTS(R)-PUBLIC.

[Default:]

{heating_tangential_history} = 'off', {tangential_rescale} = 'off',
{tangential_rotate} = 'off', {tangential_incremental} = 'off', {coulomb_total} = 'off',
{twisting_marshall} = 'off'
