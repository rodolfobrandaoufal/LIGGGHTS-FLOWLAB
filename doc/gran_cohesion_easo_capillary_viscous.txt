:line

gran cohesion easo/capillary/viscous model :h3

[Syntax:]

cohesion easo/capillary/viscous :pre

zero or more keyword/value pairs may be appended to the end (after all models are specified) :l
  {tangential_reduce} values = 'on' or 'off'
    on = the normal force of this model (capillary + viscous) is added to the normal force seen by the Coulomb limit of the tangential model
    off = the tangential model does not see the normal force computed by this model
  {easo_capillary_willett} values = 'on' or 'off'
    on = capillary force from the closed form of Willett et al. (2000) instead of the Soulie fit
    off = capillary force from the Soulie fit (default)
  {easo_wall_legacy} values = 'on' or 'off' (fix wall/gran only)
    on = legacy wall radius: the wall is treated as a sphere of the particle radius (R* = R/2)
    off = sphere-plane radius R* = R (default)
  {easo_lubrication_legacy} values = 'on' or 'off'
    on = legacy reading of minSeparationDistanceRatio: minimum gap = ratio*R* for every value
    off = a value >= 1 means minimum gap = (ratio-1)*R* (default) :pre

[Description:]

This model can be used as part of "pair gran"_pair_gran.html
It adds a liquid bridge force, caused by a surface liquid film on the particles,
to a pair of particles, which consists
of a capilary and a viscous part. Furthermore, it solves for the
transfer of surface liquid from one particle to the other as the bridge breaks up.
The model uses a parameter, {maxSeparationDistanceRatio}, to apply a cut-off
to the liquid bridge force, i.e. {radius}*{maxSeparationDistanceRatio} is
the effective contact radius of the particles. The model follows a composition 
of models suggested by "(Easo)"_#Easo

:line

Bridge formation and break-up, surface liquid transfer:

{Vbond}, the volume of surface liquid involved in the bridge, is given by
"(Shi)"_#Shi as

Vbond = 0.5*surfaceLiquidVolI*(1-sqrt(1-radj^2/(radi+radj)^2)) + 0.5*surfaceLiquidVolJ*(1-sqrt(1-radi^2/(radi+radj)^2))
surfaceLiquidVolI = surfaceLiquidContentI * 4/3*pi*radi^3 :pre

where {surfaceLiquidVolI/J} is the surface liquid volume attached to particle i/j
and {surfaceLiquidContentI/J} is its surface liquid content (a volume fraction,
see below). For two equal spheres Vbond = 0.067*(surfaceLiquidVolI+surfaceLiquidVolJ),
i.e. about 6.7 % of the liquid of each particle enters the bridge. For a
wall contact the wall is dry (surfaceLiquidVolJ = 0) and Vbond = 0.5*surfaceLiquidVolI.
This model assumes that bridge formation occurs upon contact and
the rupture distance is given as follows by "(Lian)"_#Lian :

dist0 = (1+contactAngleEff/2)*Vbond^(1/3) :pre

When the bridge breaks, it is assumed that the surface liquid volume
distributes evenly to the two particles.

:line

Capilary force:

The capilary force is given by "(Soulie)"_#Soulie as:

Fcapilary = - pi*liquidSurfaceTension(typeI,typeJ)*sqrt(radi*radj)*(exp(A*dist/R2+B)+C) :pre

where

volBondScaled = volBond/R2^3
A = -1.1*volBondScaled^(-0.53)
B = (-0.148*log(volBondScaled)-0.96)*contactAngleEff*contactAngleEff - 0.0082*log(volBondScaled) + 0.48
C = 0.0018*log(volBondScaled)+0.078;
contactAngleEff = 0.5*(contactAngleI+contactAngleJ) :pre

{dist} is the distance between the particles' surfaces, {liquidSurfaceTension} is the
liquid-vapour surface tension of the fluid (N/m), optionally per atom-type pair, {contactAngleI/J} are the contact angles for particle i/j and the fluid. {R2} is the
radius of the larger of the two particles in contact.

Wall (sphere-plane) contacts: the force is evaluated for the equivalent
pair of equal spheres with the same effective radius R* = R (Derjaguin
approximation), i.e. with radi = radj = R2 = 2*R. At contact this gives
the sphere-plane force 4*pi*R*gamma*cos(theta) times the accuracy of the
fit (the Soulie fit gives 0.87-0.95 of the theoretical contact force also
for particle pairs). Earlier versions treated the wall as a sphere of the
particle radius, which halved the wall capillary force; use
{easo_wall_legacy} = on in "fix wall/gran"_fix_wall_gran.html to restore it.

Willett option ({easo_capillary_willett} = on): the capillary force is

Fcapilary = - 2*pi*R*liquidSurfaceTension*cos(contactAngleEff)/(1 + 1.05*Shat + 2.5*Shat^2)
Shat = dist*sqrt(R/Vbond)
R = 2*radi*radj/(radi+radj) (pairs), R = 2*radi (walls) :pre

(closed form of "(Willett)"_#Willett for contact angles up to about 50
degrees). It has no force jump at rupture. Bridge volume and rupture
distance are the same as for the Soulie fit.

:line

Viscous force:

the normal and tangential parts of the viscous force are calculated as given by "(Nase)"_#Nase :

rEff = radi*radj/(radi+radj) (pairs), rEff = radi (walls)
stokesPreFactor = 6*pi*fluidViscosity*rEff
FviscN = stokesPreFactor*vn*rEff/max(dist,distMin)
FviscT = stokesPreFactor*vt*(8/15*log(rEff/max(dist,distMin))+0.9588) :pre

where {vn} and {vt} are the normal and tangential relative velocities of the
particles at the contact point, {fluidViscosity} is the viscosity of the fluid
and {ri} and {rj} are the particle radii. FviscN is the Reynolds
lubrication force 6*pi*eta*rEff^2*vn/dist ("(Pitois)"_#Pitois); for a
wall contact rEff = R (sphere-plane). The minimum gap {distMin} keeps the
viscous force finite; during overlap {dist} = {distMin} is used. It is set
by {minSeparationDistanceRatio}:

value >= 1: distMin = (minSeparationDistanceRatio - 1)*rEff, read like {maxSeparationDistanceRatio} (reduced by 1). The recommended value 1.01 gives distMin = 0.01*rEff. A value of exactly 1 is an error. :ulb,l
value < 1: distMin = minSeparationDistanceRatio*rEff (e.g. 0.01 gives 0.01*rEff). :l,ule

IMPORTANT NOTE: Earlier versions always used distMin =
minSeparationDistanceRatio*rEff. With the recommended value 1.01 the
minimum gap was then larger than any bridge length, so the viscous force
did not depend on the gap at all (lubrication switched off) and was
1-4 % of the lubrication force. Decks with a value >= 1 therefore give
different (larger, gap-dependent) viscous forces now, and a warning is
printed once. {easo_lubrication_legacy} = on restores the old reading.
Decks with a value < 1 are unchanged. Earlier versions also used rEff =
R/2 for walls (viscous wall force 1/4 of the sphere-plane value); see
{easo_wall_legacy}.

:line

Computation of liquid transport and effect of liquid content on other particle properties:

Per default, this model automatically instatiates a scalar transport equation that solved for
the surface liquid content of each particles, expressed as a volume FRACTION of the
solid volume (4/3 pi * radius ^3), i.e. 0.01 means that the liquid volume is 1 % of the
solid volume. (Earlier versions of this page said "volume %"; the code has always
used a fraction, so entering 1 for 1 % gives a 100 times too large bridge volume.) The surface liquid volume is assumed to be small, i.e. it is assumed
to have no effect on the particle mass, diameter and density.

The user can override the default behavior by explicity specifing a fix that solves
for the surface liquid transport between particles. Such fixes are
"fix liquidtransport/porous"_fix_liquidtransport_porous.html or
"fix liquidtransport/sponge"_fix_liquidtransport_sponge.html

:line

Initialization:

If you are using the this model model, you must define the following properties:
fix id all property/global minSeparationDistanceRatio scalar value
    (value=minimum lubrication gap: (value-1)*rEff for value >= 1, value*rEff for value < 1; recommended as 1.01)
fix id all property/global maxSeparationDistanceRatio scalar value
    (value=value for the maximum separation distance, recommended as 1.1)
fix id all property/global surfaceLiquidContentInitial scalar value
    (value=initial surface liquid volume as a fraction of the solid volume, e.g. 0.01 for 1 %)
fix id all property/global liquidSurfaceTension scalar value
    (value=value for the liquid-vapour surface tension of the liquid (SI units N/m))
  or
fix id all property/global liquidSurfaceTension peratomtypepair n_atomtypes value_11 value_12 .. value_21 value_22 ..
    (value_ij=liquid surface tension used for a bridge between atom types i and j (SI units N/m))
fix id all property/global fluidViscosity scalar value
    (value=value for the fluidViscosity (SI units Pas))
fix id all property/global contactAngle peratomtype value_1 value_2 ...
    (value_i=value for contact angle of atom type i and fluid, in degrees, 0 <= value_i <= 180) :pre

Property names for the liquid surface tension (read in this order of precedence):

{liquidSurfaceTension} (scalar or peratomtypepair): preferred name. A scalar applies to all type pairs. :ulb,l
{surfaceTension} (scalar or peratomtypepair): legacy name of LIGGGHTS 3.x input decks. It is still accepted and gives identical results, with a one-time deprecation warning. :l
{surfaceEnergy} (peratomtypepair): name used by an intermediate version of this model. It is still accepted with a one-time deprecation warning, but should not be used: {surfaceEnergy} is also the SOLID surface energy read by the thornton_ning and edinburgh normal models, a different physical quantity. :l,ule

If none of them is defined, the model stops with an error that names {liquidSurfaceTension}.

The optional keyword {tangential_reduce} defines if the tangential force model should
"see" the additional normal force excerted by this model. If it is 'off' (which is default)
the tangential model uses only the normal force of the normal model in its Coulomb
friction limit. If {tangential_reduce} = 'on' the capillary (attractive) and
viscous normal force of a contact in overlap is added to the normal
force before the tangential model is evaluated, which typically lowers the Coulomb
limit and thus the tangential force.

The keywords {easo_capillary_willett} and {easo_lubrication_legacy} act on the
command line where they are given ("pair_style gran"_pair_gran.html or
"fix wall/gran"_fix_wall_gran.html). {easo_wall_legacy} only has an effect on the
"fix wall/gran"_fix_wall_gran.html line, e.g.

fix wall all wall/gran model hertz tangential history cohesion easo/capillary/viscous mesh n_meshes 1 meshes plate easo_wall_legacy on :pre

[Output:]

This gran model stores a couple of per-particle properties, for access
by various "output commands"_Section_howto.html#4_15.

You can access the property surfaceLiquidContent by f_surfaceLiquidContent\[0\]
(volume fraction of the solid particle volume, dimensionless), liquidFlux (fraction of
solid particle volume/time) by accessing f_liquidFlux\[0\] and liquidSource (fraction of
solid particle volume/time) by accessing f_liquidSource\[0\]. The latter can be used to manually set a
surface liquid source via the "set"_set.html command.

Currently, there is a restriction that these properties can only be accessed
after a "run 0"_run.html command.

[Restrictions:]

This model is meant for "pair gran"_pair_gran.html. Used in
"fix wall/gran"_fix_wall_gran.html, the wall is treated as dry (no liquid on
the wall, no liquid transfer to it) and a warning is printed.

The cohesion model is not available for "atom_style"_atom_style.html superquadric
